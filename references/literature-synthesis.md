# Literature Synthesis

Use this when the task is to synthesize papers, notes, and competing research directions before writing or experimenting. The result still goes into `research-context.md`; this reference tells you what to extract and where it belongs.

## What To Pull Into The File

- In `Current Context`: the research topic, why the synthesis matters, what downstream decision it informs, the surrounding method background, the terminology, and the relevant related-work landscape.
- In `Observed Facts`: claims that are explicitly supported by the source texts.
- In `Comparisons and Tensions`: agreements, disagreements, method differences, assumption differences, and limitation contrasts.
- In `Hypotheses or Interpretive Angles`: what direction currently looks most promising, labeled as interpretation.
- In `Open Questions`: unresolved disagreements, unsupported inferences, and missing evidence.
- In `Inlined Source Materials`: paper extracts, related-work notes, terminology or method references, and result summaries with source labels.

## Comparison Grid

For each source, capture:

- central claim
- method or approach
- assumptions
- limitations
- relationship to other sources
- relevance to the current question
- what background or terminology the reader needs before interpreting the source

Avoid collapsing disagreements into a single narrative too early. Keep tension visible until the evidence actually resolves it.
